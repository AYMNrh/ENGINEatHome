# THEORY.md - ceilings for Inkling 2 Mini UD-IQ3_XXS on one RTX 2080 Ti

Method from the inference-optimisation skill (`refs/skill`, references/ceilings.md): measure the hardware, count the bytes
of the real checkpoint per emitted token, divide; prefill against ~50% of measured peak. Generated by `k_hwbench.cu`
(`results/hwbench.txt`) and `k_theory.py` (`results/theory.json`, real GGUF tensor sizes incl. padding).

### Where the gap is (reprofile after the fusions, 15.97 ms GPU time / token at 0K)
| component | bytes / token | GPU time / token | effective GB/s | % of 547 | time at 90% of ceiling | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| attention mat-vecs + LM head (Q8_0, Q5_K, Q6_K) | 1622 MB | ~4.2 ms | ~386 | 71% | 3.3 ms | ~0.9 ms |
| routed experts (IQ3_XXS, IQ2_S, NVFP4, IQ4_NL) | 1299 MB | ~5.7 ms | ~228 | 42% | 2.6 ms | **~3.1 ms** |
| loop controllers (F32 ctrl_down + ctrl_out) | 227 MB | 1.22 ms | ~186 | 34% | 0.46 ms | **~0.76 ms** |
| router + other F32 mat-vecs | ~60 MB | ~0.43 ms | ~140 | 26% | 0.12 ms | ~0.3 ms |
| glue that moves ~no weight bytes (conv, mix, norms, quantize, top-k, FA at 0K, ...) | ~0 | ~4.4 ms | - | - | ~1 ms (latency floor) | **~3.4 ms** |
| idle (token boundary, inter-kernel) | - | ~1.6 ms wall | - | - | ~0.3 ms | ~1.3 ms |
So the three big pools are: expert mat-vec efficiency (~3 ms), zero-byte glue latency (~3.4 ms), and controllers/F32 (~1 ms).
Note against the skill's lever list ("under graphs, fusing small kernels rarely helps"): here it did help (+8.7% bit-exact
from 4 fusions) because this model runs ~2,400 dependent sub-8 us kernels per token; the time really is in the glue.

## Prefill ceilings (pp512, ubatch 512)
- Linear FLOPs/token 9.77 G (+ attention 0.15 G at 512, 0.9 G at 8K, 2.1 G at 32K).
- Compute ceiling at 50% of measured INT8 peak (152 TOPS): **~7,600 tok/s at 512**, 6,700 at 8K, 5,700 at 32K.
- Bandwidth floor per ubatch: almost all 128 experts are touched by 512 tokens, so ~20.8 GB of expert banks + 1.6 GB dense per
  ubatch = ~41 ms -> **~12,500 tok/s** bound; compute is the tighter one.
- Measured 1,226 tok/s = **16% of the compute ceiling**. Nsight Compute showed why: MMQ expert tiles at 25% occupancy (48 KB tile,
  1 block/SM), INT8 pipe 8-11% active, lg_throttle from the IQ grid-table loads. ~32 tokens/expert/visit at -ub 512 starve the
  tiles (skill reference: "small per-expert row counts at a given chunk size lower GEMM efficiency; that is usually the biggest gap").

## What this says about priorities (gap x likelihood)
1. Loop controllers: 0.76 ms gap, small F32 GEMVs with a fork-specific structure (rank-64 down + gain + forget), high likelihood -> do first.
2. Glue latency: keep fusing contiguous chains (Q8_0 horizontal projections, down-scale gather, top-k + route tail).
3. Expert mat-vecs: biggest byte gap but four kernel variants already failed on IQ3_XXS dequant (instruction/MIO bound); only the
   MoE-kernel routing (opt-in, not bit-exact) helped. Revisit only with a new hypothesis (e.g. tensor-core decode of several rows).
4. Prefill: largest relative gap (16% of ceiling); needs an MMQ tile redesign for 16-64 rows per expert.
5. Long context: KV is 7% of bytes at 8K and 17% at 32K; FA vec kernel efficiency matters beyond 16K.

## Attention at depth (checkpoint 3 measurement)
| depth | KV bytes / token | FA vec decode time / token | effective | at 90% of 547 GB/s | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8K | 254 MB | 1.59 ms | 160 GB/s | 0.52 ms | ~1.1 ms |
| 32K | 682 MB | 3.52 ms | 194 GB/s | 1.39 ms | ~2.1 ms |
Nsight Compute, 8K full-attention launch: 99.7 us, DRAM 20.7 MB (the KV once), L2 reads 66 MB (3.7x), 244 regs/thread, 24%
occupancy. llama.cpp packs GQA heads only when the mask is shared across heads (mask->ne[2] == 1); Inkling's per-head relative
bias makes ne[2] = 24, so the 3 query heads of a KV head each re-load and re-dequantize it from L2 (instruction/L2 bound).
A GQA-batched q8_0 decode kernel with per-head mask rows is the identified lever (largest share of the remaining gap beyond 16K).

## Prefill MoE GEMMs: what bounds them (round 4, SASS source counters, `prof/src_iqB_*.sass.csv`)
- **Tile shape matters less than the per-weight work.**
  - J=48 instead of J=32 gives +3%: fewer mostly-empty second column tiles, each of which re-dequantizes the expert slice.
  - 2 blocks/SM is neutral. At 222–255 registers per thread the SM holds 8 warps whatever the block shape.
- **MMA phase (`vec_dot`): 55–70% of executed instructions, but IMMA is only 9–13%.**
  - Every int32 MMA partial sum is converted (I2F) and scaled (FMUL/FFMA) per 32-K (or 16-K) sub-block.
  - I2F runs at 16/clk/SM on sm_75 vs 64 for FP32. It made up 13–21% of instructions and 13–25% of stall samples.
  - Replaced with an exact IADD+FADD: +5% prefill.
  - The FP scale math is now the largest remaining part of the MMA phase.
- **Loaders.**
  - IQ: emulated byte-SIMD plus global grid lookups caused `lg_throttle` of 28–34%.
  - After the rewrite (arithmetic signs, smem grids) the IQ3_XXS kernel dropped from 2.78 to 2.50 ms. The stall moved to `mio_throttle` and LUT bank conflicts.
  - NVFP4 is latency-bound: 69% `long_scoreboard` in the loader segment. The fix needs ≤128 registers or register double-buffering.
- **pp512 now 1361 = 18% of the ~7,600 tok/s compute ceiling** (was 16%).

## Multi-token verification economics (v0.2, DSpark parked)
- Generic verify batch (target only, bin-best, depth 0): N=1 15.3 ms, 2 24.2 (1.58x), 3 29.7 (1.95x), 4 35.7 (2.34x), 5 44.3 (2.9x), 6 50.4 (3.3x), 8 47.6 (3.1x, MMQ path). About 3.8 ms per extra token.
- Break-even accepted tokens per step L = (Tv(N) + Td) / T1, with Td about 3.5 ms (estimate: 0.4 GB Q8 drafter + 201K head + launches): N2 1.81 (max 2), N3 2.17, N4 2.56, N5 3.13, N8 3.34. Reported L on a 3090 (NInfer/Maya, other engines): prose 1.5-2.6, code 2.3-2.9, edit/copy 3.7-4.4. So: marginal on code, loss on prose, win on edit/copy only.
- nsys at N=4: expert mat-vecs 17.1 ms (3.0x the N=1 5.7 ms). Per-pair time is roughly constant (0.71 ms per pair-set at N=1, 0.53 at N=4). The union of experts at N=4 is about 28 of 32 pairs for random routing, so cross-token weight reuse can remove at most ~12% (more with correlated real routing, not measured).
- Grouped-expert warp kernel (per-expert ballot grouping, one warp applies dequantized rows to all its tokens, bit-exact): slower, N=4 35.7 -> 45.6 ms. Dequant was not common-subexpression-eliminated across tokens and one warp serializing N tokens loses latency hiding. Patch: results/grp_verify_v02_rejected.patch. To revisit: register-resident dequantized weight reuse with tokens spread across warps, or tensor-core small-N tiles, and a real speculative loop to measure correlated routing.
- Drafter facts: dspark-Inkling-2-Mini-Q8_0.gguf (402 MB, arch dflash) has an rnn_hidden Markov head (dspark_fuse, dspark_hidden_out, dspark_norm, joint_proj, markov_w1 with 201024 rows, mask_embd, attn_k_ctx/v_ctx, dflare_norm). The fork dspark.cpp expects markov_w2/output instead, so it does not load. Math reference: Basalt v0.5.5 (ledger V02-REF). Rollback must be verified independently for SWA rings, conv rings (k, v, a, m) and full-attention KV.
