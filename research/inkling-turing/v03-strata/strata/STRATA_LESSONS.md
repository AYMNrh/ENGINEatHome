## 1. How Strata verifies a tree without touching committed state (src: `engine/src/inkling2/cuda/`)
- One entry point, `forward_tree_verify` (ink_runner.cuh:1478); block-8 is just a chain tree. M nodes go through all 48 visits in one CUDA graph (graph key {M, mode 2, taps, kv dtype}); production caps the tree at 12 nodes and emission at 8 (ink_cuda_backend.cu:494, 503).
- **Nothing committed is written during verify.** Attention only reads the KV caches (ink_attn.cuh:508-519). `k_ink_post_qkv_fused` in mode 2 writes only scratch and staging (`d_stage_k/v` [48, tree_cap, 1024] f32 and `d_stage_sconv` [48, tree_cap, 6656] f32, ink_runner.cuh:600-602). `k_ink_sconv4_tree` and the MoE reduce take the ShortConv history as `const` and write only staging.
- **Tree attention without a mask tensor:** `InkTreeSpec::build` gives each node an ancestor-per-depth table (`anc[i][d]`, ink_runner.cuh:36-55). A node at absolute position `p0+depth` loops keys; for `kp >= p0` it reads the staged K/V of its own ancestor at depth `kp-p0`, otherwise the committed cache (ink_attn.cuh:502-519). Relative bias uses `clamp(qp-kp, 0, 1024)`, i.e. depth difference, with a per-node table. Sliding-window reads use the ring `kp % cap_pos` and cannot be overwritten during verify because commit is a separate kernel (ring size `cap_pos` = 1536 by default, down to 1024+32..64 when VRAM is tight).
- **ShortConv for tree nodes:** a per-node lag table (`sconv_lag[i*3+l]` = ancestor index, or -1/-2/-3 = committed history slot) makes each node read its conv inputs along its ancestor chain, not the previous row (ink_runner.cuh:36-55; K:1048-1067; A:158-163). The 3-sample committed history is read-only.
- **MoE for M nodes:** router top-8 per token (ties to lowest id), then a device-side counting sort into per-expert token lists (`k_ink_moe_bin`, ink_moe_cuda.cuh:253-310). Decode-style expert kernels loop over the tokens of an active expert (`MAX_M` chunks of 4), so an expert's weights are read once per chunk, not once per token; at M >= 24 tensor-core kernels take over (not used in production, which caps M at 12).
- **Commit (two fused launches + taps), ink_spec.cuh / ink_cuda_backend.cu:518-532:** `k_ink_commit_sconv_all48` (grid 48x4) slides the 3-sample history through the accepted path's staged raw inputs; `k_ink_commit_kv_all48` (grid 48x8) writes only the accepted nodes' K/V at positions `p0..p0+n_acc-1` (ring index `p % cap_pos` for the 40 sliding-window visits, `p` for the 8 global visits; fp16 cast or fp8 per-head scale); `k_ink_compact_taps` + `ingest_taps_from` push the accepted path's taps (visits 5, 23, 41, final) into the drafter context KV. Rejected nodes cost nothing (staging is overwritten). Single stream, ordering by launch order.
- **Rollback of ShortConv only:** the backend snapshots all 48x4 histories before verify (`k_ink_sconv_slot_copy`, 2.5 us comment) to support mid-tree EOS; KV above `pos` is simply ignored and overwritten later.
- **48 visits vs 24 layers:** visits 0-5 prelude (layers 0-5), 3 passes over core layers 6-17 (visits 6-41), coda 42-47 (layers 18-23). Every visit has its own KV cache and its own ShortConv pool (48 allocations); weights are shared per physical layer. 40 sliding-window + 8 global visits. This is the same organisation as our fork.
- **Scratch VRAM (Strata):** staging 25.5 MiB at tree_cap 16, 51 MiB at 32; split-K attention partials 1.6 MB (cap 16); compact taps 0.6 MB; MoE workspace y_pair 2.4 MB + h 1.2 MB at M=32. **Drafter state is the big one:** `d_ctx_k/v` 3 layers x 2 x max_ctx x 1024 fp16 plus `d_f_rnn` [max_ctx, 2304] fp32 (ink_dspark.cuh:1014-1016, 1081-1087): about 2.8 GiB at 128K and **5.6 GiB at 256K**, plus the drafter weights (0.38 GB Q8_0 in our GGUF).
- **Parity claims:** the code carries "100% lossless/bit-exact" comments for M <= 16 (S:6, A:499, R:1547) but also shows different kernels for M=1 vs M>1: BF16 GEMV templates, MoE `<1>` vs `<4>` kernels, cuBLAS (TF32 on sm >= 80) for M > 16, tensor-core MoE at M >= 24. The reviewer could not verify bit parity on any path, and my runs show it does not hold (section 3).

## 2. What can be adapted to ggml-cuda / our fork (audit of e0be55a)
Already in our fork:
- **ShortConv rollback:** `llm_arch_supports_rs_rollback` includes Inkling (llama-arch.cpp:1503-1522); `n_rs_seq` snapshots (llama-graph.cpp:4274-4343, llama-memory-recurrent.cpp seq_rm :165-260) allow suffix rollback of up to `n_rs_seq` tokens. Cost: 3.66 MiB per sequence per snapshot slot (n_rs_seq 8: ~33 MiB).
- **KV rollback:** global KV suffix `seq_rm` always valid; the sliding-window ring has spare room of `size_swa-1024` = 256-512 cells, so rollbacks up to that length are safe (inferred from llama-kv-cache.cpp:1079-1215, untested).
- **Speculative framework:** server `n_rs_seq` plumbing (common.cpp:1748), DSpark/DFlash types in `common/speculative.cpp`, drafter file `dspark-Inkling-2-Mini-Q8_0.gguf` (arch dflash, 3 layers, block 8, taps [5,23,41,48]).
- **Therefore linear-chain verification needs no staged commit at all:** write the N verify tokens into KV in place, then `seq_rm` the rejected suffix and restore ShortConv from the snapshot. Extra memory is just the snapshot (about 33 MiB), smaller than Strata's staging.
Missing:
- **Mini DSpark head** (rnn_hidden Markov head, GRU-like update, tensor mapping, 200058 vs 201024 padded vocab rows) is not in `src/models/dspark.cpp`; this blocked the earlier attempt. Strata's `ink_dspark.cuh` (MIT) is a second reference besides Basalt.
- **Tree verification:** no tree/ancestor mask, no parent array, batch validator requires consecutive positions per sequence (llama-batch.cpp:~446-470); a tree would need one seq_id per branch, `seq_cp` of the recurrent cell (needs `n_seq_max` >= branches), equal-length ubatch splits, a tree-aware ShortConv op (per-node lag gather) and a new commit path for accepted-branch KV cells. That is a rewrite of memory/batch semantics, not a patch.
- **Cheap verify:** the main reason Strata wins decode is that a 6-node verify costs about 1.7x one token (55 ms per iteration vs 32.6 ms single token, including drafter), against 2.34x at N=4 in our generic path (measured earlier) and a rejected grouped-expert attempt (-28%). Strata's MoE reads each active expert once for all tokens; our N>1 decode path does not.

### Port cost estimates (engineering effort, not measured)
| Piece | Work | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Chain verify with existing n_rs_seq + KV seq_rm (needs drafter) | 1-2 days of wiring and tests (bit-exactness test vs plain decode needed first, see section 3) | low-medium |
| Mini DSpark head + GGUF mapping + logit validation | 3-5 days | medium (validation vs a reference; Strata engine can cross-check end-to-end acceptance) |
| Cheaper N=4..8 MoE verify (expert-grouped kernels, our earlier attempt failed) | open-ended; likely the real bottleneck | high |
| Tree verify (W=2..4): tree mask, per-branch sequences or staging+commit kernels, tree ShortConv, selective commit | 2-4 weeks | high; adds roughly +20-40% tokens/iter over a chain (Strata's tree averaged 3.6 tokens per iteration over the 12 short prompts, range 2.6-6.2, and 2.4-4.3 on the 8K/32K runs; a chain figure was not measured) |
Scratch VRAM, ours: chain snapshot ~33 MiB + drafter weights 0.38 GB + drafter context state (see below). Verification cost model (from measured numbers): iteration time = verify(N) + draft (~3 ms est.); N=5 at 2.6x of 18 ms plus 3 ms = 50 ms. At 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.3 tokens per iteration that is 60 / 70 / 86 tok/s vs 55 today (+9% / +27% / +56%); break-even ~2.8 tokens per iteration. Strata's own numbers: mean 3.6 tokens per iteration (2.4-6.2) with its W=4 tree on code continuation.

## 3. DSpark on/off numerical correctness (Strata SQ-15, RTX 2080 Ti, fp16 KV, greedy)
Method: 12 short prompts (320 tokens of the synthetic repository, 160 new tokens), spec off vs spec on. Spec-on is deterministic run to run (two identical runs, identical draft counters). For every divergence I built the common prefix (prompt + the identical generated tokens) and obtained teacher-forced logits for it from (a) a CPU FP32 reference (`--dump-ref --cpu`, 4 cases, ~4 min each) and (b) the GPU batched forward (all 12 cases), then compared logit(M=1 token) and logit(spec token).
- **12 of 12 prompts diverge** (first difference at generated token 2-58, median ~16).
- In **all 12** the spec-off (M=1) token is the argmax of the batched reference (and of the CPU FP32 reference where checked); the spec-on token was never better.
- **Near-ties (gap < 1.5 logits): 4 of 12** (gaps 0.46, 0.54, 0.79, 1.22). Measured GPU numerical noise: GPU batched vs FP32 CPU mean |error| 0.24-0.39 logits on the top-20 tokens, argmax mismatch 1.2-2.7% of positions, all with CPU top1-top2 gap < 0.3. So these 4 are consistent with ordinary floating-point near-ties.
- **Genuine errors (gap > 5 logits): 8 of 12** (gaps 5.5, 8.9, 10.0, 10.2, 10.9, 11.0, 14.0, 16.8); in two of them the spec token ranked 4046 and 4747 in the vocabulary. A 10-17 logit miss is far outside the noise (p99 error 1.4, max 3.7). Interpretation: the speculative path emitted a token that the target model does not prefer, i.e. a verification/commit/state error (or a screened-argmax error), not a tie. I did not find the cause. Candidate causes (unverified): screened `head_argmax_exact` candidate set, tree-ancestor/lag table, a row misalignment in the greedy walk, sm_75-specific kernel behaviour for M>1. Not tested on any other GPU.
- Conclusion: Strata's "100% lossless" claim does not hold in this setup. Even if it were bit-exact for the accepted tokens it would not be bit-exact against M=1 decode, because the M>1 kernels differ from the M=1 kernels (section 1), which makes near-ties flip at ~2% per position per path. **Any DSpark port must therefore be validated with a logit-level check (teacher-forced margin analysis as above), never by trusting the acceptance algorithm.** For our engine, bit-exactness against plain decode would hold only if verify reuses the identical per-token kernels (it does not today at N>1), so DSpark must be an opt-in, non-bit-exact mode.

## 4. 256K context audit and memory plan
Measured allocation probes (load only, no prompt; our e0be55a, q8_0 unless noted; GPU total 22,528 MiB; Swift stopped):
| -c / -ub / KV | loaded | used MiB | headroom MiB |
|---|---|---|---|
| 262144 / 256 / q8_0 | no: 12,768 MiB compute buffer OOM | - | - |
| 262144 / 128 / q8_0 | yes | 21,910 | 618 |
| 262144 / 64 / q8_0 | yes | 18,718 | 3,810 |
| 262144 / 256 / q4_0 | no: OOM (same 12.8 GiB buffer) | - | - |
| 262144 / 128 / q4_0 | yes | 19,812 | 2,716 |
| 131072 / 512 / q8_0 | no: 12.9 GiB buffer OOM | - | - |
| 65536 / 512 / q8_0 | yes | 18,810 | 3,718 |
Fit: **compute buffer = ~199 bytes x ub x n_kv** (ub 64 to 128 at 256K adds 3,192 MiB = 199 B x 64 x 262144; 131072x512 and 262144x256 both need 13.4-13.5 GB). KV is not the problem: global KV at 256K is 4.25 GiB q8_0 / 2.25 q4_0 / 8 f16, sliding-window KV 0.10 GiB (q8_0, ub 256), ShortConv 3.7 MiB per sequence (audit arithmetic).
Why the buffer is so large (audit, plausible but allocator reuse not observed): the graph builds the Inkling relative-bias mask as F32 `[24, n_kv, n_tok]` gather + permute chain (llama-graph.cpp:4503-4507) and our CUDA fusion (`inkling2_relbias_mask`, ggml-cuda/inkling2.cu:1890-2021) replaces it only at execution time, so ggml-alloc still reserves the unfused F32 intermediates: two live F32 tensors x 24 heads x 4 B = 192 B per (ub x n_kv) element, matching the measured 199 B. The final F16 mask the kernel actually needs is 48 B per element.
**Memory plan (concrete):**
1. Today, no code change: 256K with q8_0 KV and `-ub 64..96` (18.7-20.3 GB), or q4_0 KV at `-ub 128` (19.8 GB, 2.7 GB headroom, accuracy cost below). Prefill at ub 64 is untested and will be slower; decode is unaffected. The first-token cost of a cold 256K prefill was not measured (excluded).
2. Smallest isolated change (not implemented): emit the relbias mask as a single graph node (one new ggml op or custom node, CUDA kernel already exists and is bit-exact) when building for CUDA, keeping the old chain for other backends. Estimated compute buffer ~58 B x ub x n_kv (48 F16 mask + 4 I32 rel_pos + 2 F16 base mask + 4 F32 cast): 256K at ub 256 about 3.6 GiB instead of 12.5 GiB, total about 19.2 GiB (q8_0), ub 512 does not fit (about 22.9 GiB) without q4_0. Expected gain: usable `-ub 256` at 256K, about 4x the micro-batch of today's fitting configuration; no numerical change; probably a small prefill speedup from dropping copies. Needs test-backend-ops + decode-equivalence validation (same method as earlier commits).
3. Second candidate: build `rel_pos` (I32 [n_kv, n_tok]) on the GPU from positions or let the kernel compute the distance itself. Today it is filled on the CPU every micro-batch (llama-kv-cache.cpp:2010-2022): 67M entries (268 MB) per ub-256 batch at 256K. I suspect (not measured) this contributes to the slow long-context prefill (577 tok/s at 128K vs 1124 at 8K); measuring it needs a profile at depth, which costs long prefills, so it is deferred.
4. Later: bias saturates at distance 1024, so keys farther than 1024 have a per-head constant bias; a flash-attention variant taking a per-head far-bias constant would remove the F16 per-head mask for global visits entirely (new FA kernel, larger change).
Q8 vs Q4 vs FP8 KV:
- Q4_0 saves 2.1 GiB at 256K but is not faster (decode 48.5 vs 50.7 tok/s at 8K, 44.4 vs 45.7 at 32K) and is less accurate: greedy output diverged from q8_0 at token 2 on the 8K prompt (mean |dlogprob| 0.10 over 2 tokens) and, at 32K, 128 tokens matched but mean |dlogprob| 0.023, max 0.29. One prompt each; not conclusive for quality, enough to say q4_0 is a memory fallback, not a free win.
- F8_E4M3 is supported on our CUDA FA path (compiled combination f8_e4m3, per-layer k/v scale, fattn.cu:564-580, CMakeCache) but at 1 byte per element it is only ~6% smaller than q8_0 (1088 B per 1024-element row) with a 3-bit mantissa, so it is less accurate than q8_0 for no capacity gain. Strata uses FP8/K8V4 mainly for many-slot serving and dual-GPU.
- Mixed K q8_0 / V q4_0 (Strata's K8V4) is not compiled (`GGML_CUDA_FA_QUANTS` has only same-type pairs) and falls back to a slow f16 conversion; enabling it is a build flag in an experimental build dir, giving ~3.2 GiB KV at 256K; untested.
- No NVFP4/MXFP4 KV type exists in the fork.
Comparison with Strata's organisation: identical per-visit layout (40 ring + 8 global KV caches, per-visit ShortConv state). Differences: ring 1536 cells fixed vs ours 1024+ub rounded to 256; fp8 per-head-token scale vs our per-layer scale; their prefill attention materialises scores through cuBLAS FP16 (suspected cause of their slow 128K prefill and OOM with fp16 KV at 135K context on this card, not measured). Nothing in their KV layout saves memory over ours; the saving available to us is the compute buffer.
Interaction with DSpark: the drafter's context state is 5.6 GiB at 256K (2.8 GiB at 128K) in Strata's design, which does not fit next to 256K on 22 GB even after fix 2 (about 19.2 + 5.6 + 0.4 GiB > 22). At 128K, after fix 2 (about 17 GiB with ub 256), DSpark's ~3.2 GiB fits. A windowed drafter context would be needed for 256K.

## 5. Recommendations
1. **256K memory plan:** adopt plan step 1 as the documented configuration now; implement step 2 (single-node relbias mask) as the one next change, validated bit-exact; defer 3 and 4.
2. **Porting cost:** chain verification on our existing rollback machinery is cheap (1-2 days wiring + the Mini drafter head, 3-5 days); staged tree verification and selective commit is a 2-4 week rewrite with a high risk, and in our fork "staging" is unnecessary for chains because KV suffix removal and the 33 MiB ShortConv snapshot already give the same rollback.
3. **Is DSpark worth implementing on 22 GB?** Conditionally yes at <= 128K, no at 256K as designed. For: Strata shows a 2.2-2.5x speedup against its own plain decode and a +21-40% advantage over our current decode at 8K-32K, with a mean of 3.6 tokens per iteration; chain DSpark on our engine is projected at roughly +10% to +55% (break-even ~2.8 tokens/iter) depending on acceptance and on a cheaper N=4..8 verify that we do not have. Against: it is not bit-exact (opt-in only), Strata's implementation showed genuine verification errors in 8 of 12 divergences so we must validate at the logit level, the drafter state needs ~3 GiB at 128K and ~5.6 GiB at 256K (needs windowing), and the cheap-verify kernel is the open research problem. Suggested order: (a) fix 2 for memory, (b) measure our chain verify cost at N=4..8 on the real engine and acceptance of the official drafter via Strata as a proxy, (c) only then port the Mini head.
